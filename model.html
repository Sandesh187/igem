Mathematical Modeling

We developed a multi-scale model to predict enzyme kinetics and Vibrio natriegens population dynamics in marine environments.

01. Enzyme Kinetics (PETase)

To quantify the degradation rate of PET, we utilized the Michaelis-Menten kinetic model. We assumed the concentration of the plastic substrate is significantly higher than the enzyme concentration.

$$V = \frac{V_{max} [S]}{K_m + [S]}$$

Where:

  • \(V\): Velocity of PET degradation
  • \(V_{max}\): Maximum reaction rate
  • \(K_m\): Michaelis constant
  • \([S]\): Concentration of PET surface area

02. Microbial Growth Dynamics

We modeled the growth of V. natriegens using the Monod Equation, accounting for the limiting factor of Nitrogen in ocean water.

$$\frac{dX}{dt} = \mu_{max} \frac{S}{K_s + S} X$$

Our simulation shows that even at high salinity (3.5% NaCl), the doubling time of our engineered strain remains under 25 minutes, allowing for rapid deployment in coastal Mumbai waters.

From Model to Bench

Our model predicted that PETase expression would peak at 20°C rather than 37°C. We fed this data back to the Wet Lab team, who adjusted their incubator settings, resulting in a 40% increase in protein yield. This is a direct example of our Design-Build-Test-Learn (DBTL) cycle.
